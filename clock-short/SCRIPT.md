# "The City With Two Minute Hands" — original script v1
Facts (verify before final; sources: Wikipedia "Time in the United Kingdom", "Railway time", Bristol Corn Exchange clock):
- Before railways, every British town kept its own local solar time. Bristol is ~10 min behind London (2°35'W ≈ 10 min 20 s).
- Trains made this a mess: a timetable had to quote London time while station clocks showed local time.
- Great Western Railway adopted London time on all stations from Nov 1840; the Railway Clearing House recommended
  Greenwich time for all railways in Sept 1847; nearly all companies followed by December 1847 ("railway time").
- Bristol Corn Exchange clock has TWO minute hands: one for local time, one for London time (still there).
- Law made Greenwich time the legal time across Britain only in 1880 (Statutes (Definition of Time) Act).

Hook line: In eighteen forty-seven, Britain had to agree what time it was.
